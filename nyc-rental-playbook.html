1 · Vetting a listing (the checklist)

The tool does this in one shot: node ~/Downloads/nyc-apartment-hunt/scripts/aptcheck.cjs "<url>" → scrapes listing + HPD + commute + verdict. (Cross-check commute on Google Maps — the tool takes the API's default route, which can lowball the fastest option.)
  • HPD violations — look up the building. NYC OpenData wvxf-dwi5. Open count + Class C (immediately hazardous) + how recent. 30+ Class C or 100+ open = slumlord 0-5 open = clean
  • Bed-bug registry — bedbugregistry.com/metro/nyc
  • Listing history on StreetEasy — nice apartments lease fast (few days on market). Long/repeated relisting = something's off. A big unexplained price drop = bait or defect.
  • Read the description, not the metadata — scummy brokers tag a studio as "1BR." Photos labeled "same line" aren't the actual unit.
  • Rent-stabilization check — you can't sign away the right. Cross-check the building list (hcr.ny.gov). Stabilized = capped increases + renewal right. 4 years to dispute.
  • Management company / landlord — Google them. Owner-direct & small landlords >> big corporate brokers for a no-credit applicant.

2 · Scam / red-flag detection

If it's too good to be true, it's 99% a scam. NEVER rent without physically (or via live video) seeing the unit. Never wire money / Venmo / Western Union / MoneyGram before a verified tour.
  • SCAM "Owner is abroad, check it through the windows, mail a deposit and I'll send keys."
  • SCAM Generic Gmail + YouTube-Shorts videos + no address + demands exact credit score/income upfront = lead-harvesting (e.g. the rentalsny21@gmail.com one).
  • BAIT & SWITCH Broker won't give the exact address, wants to meet at an intersection, then "that one just rented, but I have others."
  • WATCH Broker asks you to sign paperwork at the showing giving them exclusive right to charge a fee. Read it.
  • WATCH Eager, fast-responding broker on a cheap listing — the good buildings have 50 applicants and don't chase you. Fast chasers are often the bad ones.
  • Verify ownership before any money: live video walkthrough showing them inside the unit with keys.

3 · Money rules

ItemRule (NY law / norm)
40x ruleAnnual income ≥ 40× monthly rent. $2,350 rent → $94k. Qualifies on GROSS rent, not NER.
GuarantorMust make 80x rent. Can be relative or friend (most owner-direct accept friends; some corp require relatives).
Security depositCapped at 1 month (NY law). Returned within 14 days of move-out.
Application feeCapped at $20 by law. Credit report should be ≤$30. $150 ask = run.
Rent increase noticeLandlord must give 30 days' notice for increases over 5%.
Broker fee (FARE Act, NYC)Whoever hires the broker pays. Landlord-hired = no tenant fee. NJ has no FARE Act — JC brokers can charge you.
NER (net effective rent)"2 months free on 13-mo lease." You pay GROSS in non-free months; 40x & renewal use GROSS, not the discounted NER. Don't sign NER if you can't afford the gross.

4 · No-credit / guarantor strategy (Bill-critical)

Bill has no US credit. The credit field is his weak spot. Strategy: lead with income + guarantor, never volunteer "no credit" without the offset in the same breath.
  • Offsets to offer: signed EY offer letter ($94k), US-citizen guarantor at 80x, bank statements / proof of funds, willingness to prepay extra months.
  • Target owner-direct landlords — they make judgment calls. Corporate brokers run rigid credit filters that auto-reject empty credit.
  • Backup if guarantor rejected: Insurent / TheGuarantors (institutional guarantor, ~70-110% of one month's rent, one-time fee).
  • Alt paths for no-credit/no-guarantor: NYFSC senior home-sharing program (one renter used it explicitly because "no credit history or guarantors"); co-living (Common, Outpost, Tribe — usually no guarantor/credit req, furnished, 30-day min) — but pricier & room-share.
  • Pre-stage guarantor docs NOW — when an owner says yes you have ~24-48h. Have their pay stubs + 1040 + bank statement + ID ready.

6 · What to check at a viewing (video or in person)

  • Water pressure — turn on the shower. Don't be shy.
  • Flush + shower temperature test.
  • Electric meter access — make sure ConEd can read it (retail-below story: estimate-bill nightmare).
  • Super — on-site or live-in? Or 30 min away covering 10 buildings (= slow repairs)?
  • VISIT AT NIGHT & ON WEEKENDS — neighborhood changes. (For Bill: ask current tenants / check at different times via video.)
  • Construction/demolition nearby? If yes, ask for a discount.
  • Cell service (check your bars), nearest laundromat/bodega/train, mattress fit, closet space.
  • Photos/video of everything on move-in — protects your deposit.
  • 1st floor vs ground floor: "1st floor" can be above a ground-level lobby (fine). True ground/garden = noise + security + light concerns, but can mean a backyard. Verify which.

7 · Document folder to pre-stage

Keep multiple copies (PDF in Google Drive) ready to send instantly:
  • Signed EY offer letter (income proof — Bill's substitute for pay stubs)
  • Bank statements (3 recent) — proof of funds
  • Passport / EAD card / I-20
  • Guarantor packet: their pay stubs (3), 1040 tax return, bank statement, ID
  • (When employed) W2, pay stubs, employment letter, prior-landlord reference

8 · Lease & negotiation

  • Demand a 2nd-year option at a predetermined rent (1yr + option). Don't trust "landlord will offer option later." Protects against increases. If they refuse, you dodged a bullet.
  • Negotiate the landlord to cover 50% of extra fees — they sometimes do it as good faith.
  • Keep applying while your app is out — leverage; landlords play apps against each other.
  • You're choosing a landlord, not just an apartment — owner-direct condos/small landlords want stable long-term tenants and squeeze less.
  • Read every clause; get verbal promises in writing ("if it's not written down, it doesn't exist"). Scan & store 2 copies + cloud.

9 · Bill's decision framework

Filter on sight: rental (not co-op/condo) · 12-month lease · ≤$2,350 gross · owner-direct preferred · clean HPD (run aptcheck) · ≤50 min commute · guarantor accepted.
Auto-skip: co-op/condo (board) · co-living room-share (unless desperate) · student dorms (EHS) · 100+ HPD or 30+ Class C · predatory brokers (Assist NJ, Zara Realty, Nooklyn-bad-buildings) · Elmhurst/East Elmhurst (slumlord-dense + bus commute) · NER you can't afford at gross.

When choosing between finalists: owner-direct + clean + fast commute wins, because approval ease (guarantor) matters more than amenities given the no-credit situation. A 1BR at studio price (Woodside $1,895, 21-13 25th Rd $1,950) beats a bigger/fancier place you can't get approved for or that's 50+ min away.
